\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Price changes, order-book messages and trades arrive as streams of events in continuous time,
and temporal point processes are the standard way to model them. They are used for two rather
different purposes. In econometrics, Hawkes processes \citep{hawkes1971spectra} are used to
\emph{measure} market mechanisms. Their branching structure separates exogenous events, such as
a reaction to news, from endogenous events triggered by earlier trading, and the fitted kernels
describe how quickly information is absorbed
\citep{hardiman2013critical,filimonov2012quantifying,rambaldi2015modeling}. In machine learning,
neural temporal point processes are used to \emph{predict} the next event, and they are compared
by their held-out likelihood on shared benchmarks
\citep{du2016recurrent,mei2017neural,zuo2020transformer,shchur2020intensity,xue2024easytpp,chang2025deep}.

Each use has a weakness. Measurement relies on non-convex fits whose optimality is unknown, and
on summaries that ignore part of the response. A common example is the ``half-life''
$\ln 2/\beta$ of an exponential news kernel with decay rate $\beta$, which describes the direct
reaction to news but not the further trading that this reaction sets off. On the prediction
side, most intensity-based neural models estimate the compensator $\int\lambda(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$
by Monte Carlo \citep{mei2017neural,zuo2020transformer,yang2022transformer,chang2025deep}, so
their reported likelihoods contain sampling error. Models with an exact likelihood do exist.
FullyNN \citep{omi2019fully} models the cumulative hazard with a monotone network, and
IntensityFree \citep{shchur2020intensity} models the density of the next gap directly. Both,
however, give up the Hawkes structure that makes a model interpretable \citep{shchur2021neural}.
We also find that reported likelihoods can depend on implementation details.

In this paper we show that a single algebraic object serves both purposes with exact
likelihoods. We call it a \emph{phase-type state space}: every kernel is a nonnegative
combination of Erlang densities whose rates lie on a logarithmic grid of time scales. Between
two events such a state evolves by an explicit matrix exponential, so intensities and their
integrals are available in closed form. The class is also rich, since phase-type densities are
dense in the set of densities on $\R_+$.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Certified measurement (MSX).} We define a multiscale phase-type Hawkes process
with marked news kernels in which every parameter enters the intensity linearly. Its
log-likelihood is therefore concave. We compute the global maximum with accelerated EM followed
by a barrier Newton step, and each fit comes with a Fenchel duality gap that bounds its distance
from the optimum (Proposition~\ref{prop:gap}).
\item \textbf{Echo-corrected responses in closed form.} Embedding the process in a linear
system gives the mean response to a shock as $C e^{\mathcal A t}\bm z_0$. We prove that this
system is stable exactly when the spectral radius of the branching matrix is below one
(Theorem~\ref{thm:stab}), and we obtain absorption times, echo amplification and price-discovery
curves without simulation.
\item \textbf{Exact neural prediction (EPT-X).} We introduce a neural point process whose latent
state is phase-type. It combines a linear Hawkes backbone with a history-dependent renewal
channel, a mixture of Erlang distributions that also allows ``no event'', added as a competing
risk. The likelihood remains exact, the model contains the Hawkes backbone as a special case,
and the renewal channel can approximate any conditional distribution of the next gap
(Proposition~\ref{prop:eptx}). Its states follow an affine recursion, so training uses a parallel
scan (Lemma~\ref{lem:scan}), and it can start from the certified MSX fit, which gives it a
validation likelihood no lower than that of MSX (Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}).
\item \textbf{Benchmarks and two findings about the data.} EPT-X has the best likelihood on all
five LOBSTER order-book streams and on Retweet and Taobao, and it is level with the best published
model on StackOverflow. A widely used IntensityFree implementation replaces every inter-event time
below $10^{-5}$\,s by $10^{-5}$\,s before scoring; scored on the observed gaps, it loses its lead on
two order-book streams. The gaps of the EasyTPP Amazon data are uniform on two fixed intervals,
which limits what its time likelihood can show.
\item \textbf{Application.} We study six markets around 670 scheduled macroeconomic releases
between 2022 and 2026, with a placebo test built into the likelihood. The direct reaction to
CPI, payrolls, PPI and the FOMC statement is absorbed in about a second, but the market's
subsequent trading stretches half of the response to \tEchoLo--\tEchoHi{}\,s and ninety percent
of it to \tNinetyLo--\tNinetyHi{} minutes.
\end{enumerate}

The ingredients are not new on their own. Linear-in-parameters kernel dictionaries
\citep{bacry2015hawkes}, EM algorithms for Hawkes processes
\citep{veen2008estimation,zhou2013learning}, Erlang-chain embeddings and exact-likelihood neural
point processes \citep{omi2019fully,shchur2020intensity} have all been studied. What we add is
their combination: certified, closed-form measurement on one side, an exact neural model that
contains the linear Hawkes process and starts from it on the other, and experiments showing that
both work.
