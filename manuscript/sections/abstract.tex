How fast do prices absorb scheduled macroeconomic news, and how much of the post-release
activity is the market reacting to itself? We model $\delta$-crossing price events of six
markets---EUR/USD, USD/JPY, gold, the S\&P~500, bitcoin and ether---around 670 scheduled
releases (2022--2026) with a multiscale phase-type Hawkes process whose parameters all
enter linearly. The log-likelihood is then concave, and we compute the global maximum
with a Fenchel duality-gap certificate. A Markov embedding gives the echo-corrected
response to a release in closed form; we prove it is stable iff the spectral radius of
the branching matrix is below one. A built-in placebo test on matched release-free days,
with a penalty chosen on held-out windows, separates genuine news effects from artefacts:
CPI, payrolls, PPI, the FOMC statement and retail sales pass it, second-tier releases and
the ECB and BoJ do not. For the releases that pass, the direct reaction is absorbed in
about a second, but echoes stretch half of the response to \tEchoLo--\tEchoHi{}\,s and
ninety percent to \tNinetyLo--\tNinetyHi{} minutes; the branching ratio is
$\rho(G)=\rhoPooled$ and declines from 2022 to 2026. On synthetic benchmarks the certified
estimator recovers multiscale and network kernels with 7--10$\times$ smaller likelihood
deficits than six classical estimators. We also introduce EPT-X, a neural point process
with an exact compensator that superposes a history-conditioned hyper-Erlang renewal
channel on a linear Hawkes backbone; on LOBSTER limit-order-book data it achieves the best
test likelihood on all five stocks against six classical estimators and seven neural
baselines, by \trackCmarginLo--\trackCmarginHi{} nats per event over the best neural model.
Along the way we find that a widely used IntensityFree implementation clamps inter-event
times before scoring, which overstates its likelihood on streams with many short gaps.
