\section{EPT and EPT-X: neural point processes with an exact compensator}\label{sec:ept}

We now turn to prediction. Events arrive at times $t_1<t_2<\dots$ with marks
$y_n\in\{1,\dots,M\}$, and we write $\Delta_n=t_n-t_{n-1}$ for the gaps. For a time $t$ after the
$n$-th event, $s=t-t_n$ denotes the time elapsed since that event.

\paragraph{EPT: a neural phase-type state.} The latent state $\bm\xi(t)\in\R_{\ge0}^P$ consists of
$L$ channels of Erlang chains of order $R$, each with its own learnable rate. Between events it
evolves exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}. At each event, a recurrent network summarises the
history: a GRU \citep{cho2014learning} receives an embedding of the mark $y_n$ and log transforms of
the gap $\Delta_n$ and produces a hidden vector $\bm h_n$. In EPT-X the gap enters through its
standardised logarithm, which keeps nanosecond-scale gaps distinguishable from zero. The state
then jumps to
\[
\bm\xi(t_n^+)=\sigma(W_g\bm h_n)\odot\bm\xi(t_n^-)+\mathrm{softplus}(W_\xi\bm h_n),
\]
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function, $\odot$ the elementwise product and $W_g,W_\xi$ learned
matrices. The first term lets the network forget part of the old state; the second adds a
nonnegative jump. For $t\in(t_n,t_{n+1}]$ the intensity of mark $y$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ept}
\lambda^{\mathrm{EPT}}_y(t)=\mu_y+\sum_{p=1}^{P}C_{yp}\,\xi_p(t)
+g_{1y}\,e^{-\alpha s}+g_{2y}\bigl(1-e^{-\kappa s}\bigr)
+\sum_{q}\Theta_{yq}\,\xi^{\mathrm{H}}_q(t).
\end{equation}
The quantities $\mu_y$, $C_{yp}$, $g_{1y}$, $g_{2y}$, $\alpha$ and $\kappa$ are nonnegative outputs
of the network computed from $\bm h_n$, so they stay fixed until the next event. The two hazard
terms let the intensity decay or rise with $s$, but stay bounded. An unbounded Gompertz term would
overflow on heavy-tailed gaps. The last term is a \emph{linear multivariate phase-type Hawkes
process}: $\bm\xi^{\mathrm{H}}(t)$ collects Erlang chains on a fixed logarithmic grid of rates, one
group per source mark, and every event of mark $y$ adds $\beta_k$ to the first phase of the chains
of source $y$. Its weights $\Theta_{yq}\ge0$ are learned. This term is a residual path: when the
neural read-outs are zero, EPT \emph{is} the Hawkes process of Section~\ref{sec:model}, so the network
only has to learn departures from linear excitation.

\subsection{EPT-X: a renewal channel}\label{sec:eptx}
 Order-book and social streams contain cascades, several events in
a very short interval followed by a pause. A linear Hawkes intensity describes excitation
\emph{between} cascades well, but it has no direct handle on the distribution of gaps
\emph{within} them. EPT-X therefore adds a second channel that models the next gap directly.

We fix $Q$ Erlang ``atoms'' indexed by $q$, each with an order $R_q$ and a rate $\beta_q$. Their means
$R_q/\beta_q$ lie on a logarithmic grid that spans the time scales of the data, and their orders
come from $\{1,4,16\}$, optionally up to $1024$. An atom of order $R_q$ has coefficient of variation
$R_q^{-1/2}$, so high orders give sharp peaks in log-time. From $\bm h_n$ the network outputs:
\begin{itemize}
\item weights $\pi_1,\dots,\pi_Q,\pi_\infty\ge0$ summing to one, where $\pi_\infty$ is the weight of a
\emph{defect} atom meaning ``the renewal channel produces no event'';
\item for each time scale, a distribution $\omega_{y\mid q}$ over the marks, so that the mark of a
burst may depend on how quickly it follows the last event;
\item optionally, a bounded shift of each log-rate, which lets an atom move within its grid cell.
\end{itemize}
The renewal channel's joint density of the next gap $s$ and mark $y$, and its survival function,
are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal}
p(s,y)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\pi_q\,\omega_{y\mid q}\,
\frac{\beta_q^{R_q}\,s^{R_q-1}e^{-\beta_q s}}{(R_q-1)!},\qquad
S_{\mathrm{ren}}(s)=\pi_\infty+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\pi_q\,e^{-\beta_q s}\sum_{i=0}^{R_q-1}\frac{(\beta_q s)^i}{i!}.
\end{equation}
EPT-X adds the corresponding hazard to EPT:
\[
\lambda_y(t)=\lambda^{\mathrm{EPT}}_y(t)+\frac{p(s,y)}{S_{\mathrm{ren}}(s)} .
\]
Figure~\ref{fig:eptx} shows how the parts fit together. The network only acts at event times;
between events every term evolves in closed form.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:eptx}
Let $\Lambda^{\mathrm{EPT}}(\Delta)=\int_{t_n}^{t_n+\Delta}\sum_y\lambda^{\mathrm{EPT}}_y(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$
be the compensator of EPT over a gap of length $\Delta$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The compensator of EPT-X over the same gap is $\Lambda^{\mathrm{EPT}}(\Delta)-\log S_{\mathrm{ren}}(\Delta)$,
and both terms are in closed form.
\item If $\pi_\infty=1$, EPT-X coincides with EPT, which in turn contains the linear phase-type
Hawkes process.
\item Given $\bm h_n$, the gap produced by the renewal channel is the absorption time of a finite
continuous-time Markov chain, so its survival function, quantiles and moments are closed-form
matrix expressions.
\item As the grid of atoms is refined, the renewal channel can approximate any conditional
distribution of the next gap.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part 1.} The intensity of EPT-X is a sum of two intensities, so its compensator is the sum of
the two compensators. For the renewal channel, $\sum_y p(s,y)=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}S_{\mathrm{ren}}(s)$,
because each Erlang density is the negative derivative of its survival function. Hence
\[
\int_0^{\Delta}\frac{\sum_y p(s,y)}{S_{\mathrm{ren}}(s)}\,\mathrm{d}s
=-\int_0^{\Delta}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\log S_{\mathrm{ren}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
=-\log S_{\mathrm{ren}}(\Delta),
\]
using $S_{\mathrm{ren}}(0)=1$. The EPT part is closed form by Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion} and by direct
integration of the two exponential hazard terms.

\emph{Part 2.} If $\pi_\infty=1$, all other weights are zero. Then $p\equiv0$ and $S_{\mathrm{ren}}\equiv1$,
so the renewal hazard vanishes. Setting the neural read-outs of EPT to zero leaves the Hawkes term.

\emph{Part 3.} An Erlang distribution of order $R$ is the absorption time of a chain of $R$
exponential phases. A mixture of such chains, entered at random according to the weights
$\pi_q$, together with an absorbing ``never'' state of weight $\pi_\infty$, is again a finite
continuous-time Markov chain with one absorbing state.

\emph{Part 4.} Mixtures of Erlang distributions are dense in the distributions on $(0,\infty)$
\citep{tijms1994stochastic,lee2010modeling}. The renewal channel is such a mixture, with weights
that depend on the history. This is the same expressiveness as a conditional mixture density
\citep{shchur2020intensity}, with the difference that the intensity and compensator stay exact.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Training and prediction.} We maximise the exact log-likelihood
$\sum_n\log\lambda_{y_n}(t_n)-\Lambda$, where $\Lambda$ is the total compensator, so the gradients
contain no sampling noise. For large orders we evaluate $S_{\mathrm{ren}}$ through the regularised
upper incomplete gamma function, and we take care that its gradient stays finite at zero gaps. The
log-likelihood splits exactly into a time term, $\log\lambda(t_n)-\Lambda$ with
$\lambda=\sum_y\lambda_y$, and a mark term, $\log\bigl(\lambda_{y_n}(t_n)/\lambda(t_n)\bigr)$, which we
report separately. Predictions of the next event are Bayes-optimal functionals of the exact
density: the expected gap is $\int_0^\infty S(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$, where $S$ is the survival function
of the full model, and the predicted mark is $\arg\max_y\int_0^\infty\lambda_y(s)S(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$.
Both integrals are computed by one-dimensional quadrature on a logarithmic grid.

\paragraph{Parallel evaluation.} The encoder reads only the observed gaps and marks, never the
latent state. All summaries $\bm h_1,\dots,\bm h_N$ can therefore be computed first, in one call to
the recurrent network. Given them, the state updates are affine.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:scan}
Let $E(\Delta)$ be the block-diagonal transfer matrix of the Erlang chains over a gap $\Delta$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}), and let $D_n=\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\sigma(W_g\bm h_n)\bigr)E(\Delta_n)$ and
$\bm b_n=\mathrm{softplus}(W_\xi\bm h_n)$. Then $\bm\xi(t_n^+)=D_n\bm\xi(t_{n-1}^+)+\bm b_n$, and all
post-event states follow from a prefix scan in $O(\log N)$ parallel steps. Every entry of every
$D_n$ lies in $[0,1]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first claim restates the jump rule after evolving the state over the gap by Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}.
Affine maps compose associatively, $(D_2,\bm b_2)\circ(D_1,\bm b_1)=(D_2D_1,\,D_2\bm b_1+\bm b_2)$,
so a parallel prefix scan applies. The entries of $E(\Delta)$ are Poisson probabilities and the gates
lie in $(0,1)$, so the entries of $D_n$ lie in $[0,1]$ and the scan cannot overflow.
\end{proof}

The Hawkes state follows the same scan without gates. Once the post-event states are known, the
intensity and compensator of every gap are evaluated independently. The likelihood is the same as
with the event loop; only the order of computation changes.

\paragraph{Residual mark law.} The compensator depends only on the total intensity
$\lambda=\sum_y\lambda_y$. The split of $\lambda$ across marks can therefore be made more flexible
without losing exactness. We set
\[
\tilde\lambda_y(t)=\lambda(t)\,\mathrm{softmax}_y\bigl(\log\lambda_y(t)+m_y(\bm h_n,s)\bigr),
\]
where $m$ is a small network of the history summary and the elapsed time $s$. The total intensity,
and hence the compensator, is unchanged. With $m=0$ the marks are those of EPT-X, so this is a
residual correction. Separate models for times and marks were used before by
\citet{shchur2020intensity} and \citet{panos2024decomposable}; here the time model keeps its Hawkes
structure.

\paragraph{Data-adaptive atoms.} A logarithmic grid spends atoms evenly over all time scales. When
the gaps concentrate in narrow bands, few atoms fall inside them. We therefore add atoms at $Q$
equal-mass quantiles of the training gaps. The order of each such atom is set so that its
coefficient of variation matches the log-distance $d_q$ to its nearest neighbour,
$R_q=\min\{\lceil d_q^{-2}\rceil,4096\}$. Atoms are then sharpest where the data are densest, much
like an adaptive-bandwidth kernel estimate, and the compensator stays exact.

\paragraph{A certified starting point.} The Hawkes backbone of EPT-X can use the dictionary of an
MSX fit, and MSX gives its global optimum with a certificate (Proposition~\ref{prop:gap}).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:floor}
Initialise EPT-X with the backbone weights $\Theta$ and baselines $\mu$ of the MSX optimum on the
training data, and with the neural read-outs, hazard terms and renewal weights at a scale
$\varepsilon>0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item as $\varepsilon\to0$, the initial log-likelihood of EPT-X on any data converges to that of the
MSX fit, which is within the certified gap of the best linear phase-type Hawkes process on this
dictionary;
\item if the model is selected by validation likelihood over all training epochs including the
initial one, its validation likelihood is at least that of the initial model.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part 1.} At $\varepsilon=0$ the read-outs, hazards and renewal weights vanish, so EPT-X equals
the backbone (Proposition~\ref{prop:eptx}, part 2), whose intensity and compensator are those of
MSX. The intensities and compensators depend continuously on $\varepsilon$ and are bounded below by
$\mu>0$, so the log-likelihood converges. The second statement is Proposition~\ref{prop:gap}.

\emph{Part 2.} The selected model maximises validation likelihood over a set that contains the
initial model.
\end{proof}
