\section{Specification history and the placebo test}\label{app:history}

We report the specifications that \emph{failed} the built-in falsification test, because
the failures are informative and because the final specification was chosen by them, not
by the results we hoped for. All superseded outputs are kept in
\texttt{results/json/superseded/}.

\paragraph{Version 1.} News kernels with time scales up to 30 minutes and Erlang order 3
(mean lags up to 90 minutes), a constant baseline per window, time-of-day hats in UTC, and
events that could be tied (a trade sweeping several $\delta$ levels produced several events
with the same millisecond timestamp). The single PLACEBO type absorbed \vOnePlacebo{} extra
events per pseudo-release---comparable to real releases---and crypto residuals had KS
statistics up to \vOneKSmax. Diagnosis: news kernels longer than the post-release window
acted as within-window trend absorbers, and ties (55\% of BTC events) violated the
orderliness of a simple point process.

\paragraph{Version 2.} One event per timestamp (with a size mark), news kernels restricted
to 0.1--300\,s, a per-window \emph{linear} baseline, and New-York-time seasonality. The
placebo mass fell to \vTwoPlacebo{} and residual fit improved by an order of magnitude, but
the placebo remained significant under Wald standard errors. Splitting the placebo by
release slot showed placebo mass at every slot, including 14:00\,ET on non-FOMC days, which
ruled out ``unmodelled 08:30 releases'' as the whole explanation.

\paragraph{Version 3 (final).} Two further changes. (i) Placebo days must avoid sixteen
second-tier US releases (PCE, durable goods, trade, housing, JOLTS, sentiment, \dots) as
well as the modelled ones. (ii) Nonnegative news kernels are estimated with an
exposure-weighted $\ell_1$ penalty whose level is chosen by the profile likelihood of
held-out windows (Table~\ref{tab:l1}); without it, each of the several hundred nonnegative
weights has positive expected value under the null, so release and placebo masses are
equally inflated (boundary bias). The held-out likelihood peaks at
$\ell_1=\lOneSelected$, where release and placebo masses separate.

\section{EPT ablations}
\paragraph{Hawkes backbone.} Without the explicit linear Hawkes state, EPT reached
\eptNoBackbone{} nats per event on LOBSTER AAPL (three seeds, 100 epochs), below the
classical MSX estimator; the network had to rediscover mark-specific linear excitation
through its gated jumps and did not within the epoch budget. With the backbone---a residual
path that makes the model exactly MSX when the neural read-outs vanish---EPT exceeds every
neural baseline on all five stocks and MSX on three of them (Table~\ref{tab:track-c}).

\paragraph{Renewal channel and encoder.} Adding the hyper-Erlang renewal channel (EPT-X)
improves EPT on every LOBSTER stock and on every public dataset where both were run. On
validation, sharper Erlang atoms (orders up to 1024) help where the gap law has sharp
modes---by 1.1 nats per event on Amazon, whose gaps concentrate in a narrow band, and by
0.05 on INTC---and neither a three-layer residual encoder nor a 128-unit
encoder improves any dataset (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
