\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

We test the two models in turn. Synthetic data with known ground truth show what MSX recovers.
Public benchmarks and order-book streams then compare EPT-X with published and re-run baselines,
and ablations show where its gains come from. All configuration choices are made on validation
data, and the test split is scored once.

\subsection{Synthetic recovery}\label{sec:synthetic}
We simulate five ground truths by exact cluster sampling. Several of them lie deliberately
outside the MSX class:
\begin{itemize}
\item S1: a single exponential kernel;
\item S2: a power-law (Lomax) kernel with tail exponent $1.5$;
\item S3: a gamma-shaped delayed peak plus a fast exponential;
\item S4: a sparse four-dimensional network whose kernels have components at 10\,ms and 30\,s;
\item S5: a sparse ten-dimensional network of power-law kernels.
\end{itemize}
Each scenario has ten windows per split and five seeds. We compare MSX with the exponential and
sum-of-exponential maximum-likelihood estimators, the nonparametric EM, ADM4, the conditional-law
method and NPHC. MSX-auto chooses its Erlang order and $\ell_1$ level on held-out training
windows.

Table~\ref{tab:track-a} reports how far each method's held-out log-likelihood falls below that of
the true model. When the truth is a single exponential, the estimators that assume exactly this
form are best. Under a power law, the sum-of-exponential estimators and MSX
perform about equally. When the kernel has structure that no single family captures (a delayed
peak, two separated time scales or a power-law network), MSX-auto has the smallest deficit, 7 to
10 times smaller than the best competitor's. On the two network scenarios it also roughly halves
the best competitor's branching-matrix error and recovers the causal graph perfectly (AUC $=1$,
as do several competitors). On the delayed peak, the nonparametric EM estimates the branching
matrix slightly better (Table~\ref{tab:track-a-full}). Over all 100 MSX fits, the certificate of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gap} was at most $3.3\times10^{-4}$ nats.

\subsection{Public temporal point process benchmarks}\label{sec:public}
We now turn to prediction and compare EPT-X with published neural models. We use the EasyTPP splits of Amazon, Retweet, Taxi, Taobao and StackOverflow and follow the
protocol of \citet{chang2025deep}. The metric is the log-likelihood per scored event, with the
first event of each sequence used only for conditioning, and models are selected on validation
likelihood. Published numbers are taken from \citet{chang2025deep} and \citet{boyd2025hyper}, who
use the same splits and protocol. To check our evaluation code, we re-ran S2P2 on Taxi and obtained
0.524, against 0.522 in the paper. EPT-X reports the exact likelihood.

EPT-X has the best likelihood on three of the five datasets (Table~\ref{tab:track-b}). On Retweet,
the largest dataset with 2.2 million training events, it reaches $\eptxLLRetweet\pm\eptxSDRetweet$
against $-6.348$ for the best published model, with a 25-epoch budget imposed by compute. On Taobao,
starting from the certified MSX fit (Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}) raises EPT-X to
$\eptxLLTaobao\pm\eptxSDTaobao$, against IntensityFree's published $1.318\pm0.017$. On
StackOverflow, the residual mark law gives $\eptxLLStackOverflow\pm\eptxSDStackOverflow$ against
S2P2's $-2.163\pm0.009$, so the two are within one published standard deviation.

On Taxi, EPT-X reaches $\eptxLLTaxi\pm\eptxSDTaxi$ against $0.522\pm0.004$ for S2P2 and HHP, and
its mark term equals that of our S2P2 re-run ($-0.210$ against $-0.211$).

Amazon needs a separate comment. In the EasyTPP release every gap lies in one of two intervals,
$[0.010,0.015]$ (\amzShortShare\% of the test gaps) or $[0.70,0.80]$, and within each interval the
gaps are uniform; Kolmogorov--Smirnov tests do not reject uniformity at the 1\% level on any split.
The timestamps were evidently resampled during preprocessing. A history-free density made of the
two boxes already reaches a time log-likelihood of \amzBoxLL{} nats per event, so on Amazon the
time term mostly measures how sharply a model resolves two hard edges and how well it predicts
which interval comes next. Smooth densities, such as Erlang or log-normal mixtures, lose at the
edges. Data-adaptive atoms and the certified start raise EPT-X to
$\eptxLLAmazon\pm\eptxSDAmazon$, second behind S2P2. We did not add box-shaped components, because
they would fit a preprocessing artefact rather than event dynamics.

The linear MSX model comes close to the neural models on Taobao and Retweet, whose dynamics are
largely self-exciting.

\paragraph{Tuning budgets.} We selected the EPT-X configuration on validation data from a search
over Erlang orders, grid size, encoder depth and width, an attention encoder, batch size, weight
decay and the three new components, with one seed per variant (Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and
Appendix~\ref{app:details}). The published baselines, in contrast, carry their authors' own tuning,
and our order-book baselines use the authors' EasyTPP configurations with one seed each. The
margins on Retweet, Taobao and the order books are large compared with any seed-to-seed variation
we observed.

\subsection{Limit-order-book event streams}\label{sec:lob}
Order-book streams are a harder regime than the public benchmarks: events arrive in bursts down to
microsecond gaps, across six event types. We use the public LOBSTER \citep{huang2011lobster} samples of AAPL, AMZN, GOOG, INTC and MSFT
from 21 June 2012, with six event types, a chronological 60/20/20 split and sequences of 128
events. EasyTPP rejects tied timestamps, so we displace tied messages by a uniform offset in
$(0,100\,\text{ns})$, and every model is trained and scored on these same files. The neural
baselines run in EasyTPP with the S2P2 authors' configurations, with batches of 32 to 64 instead
of 256 to fit an 8\,GB GPU. Each is trained with a 100-epoch budget, and the strongest ones also
with longer budgets. We report each at the budget with the best validation likelihood, so the
longer budgets can only help them.

EPT-X is the best model on every stock (Table~\ref{tab:track-c}). It is ahead of MSX-auto, EPT and
all seven neural baselines, and ahead of the best neural baseline by \trackCmarginLo{} to
\trackCmarginHi{} nats per event.

One baseline needs a correction. EasyTPP's IntensityFree replaces every gap below $10^{-5}$\,s by
$10^{-5}$\,s before evaluating its log-normal mixture, which can then put an arbitrarily narrow
component on that value. The result is not the likelihood of the observed gaps. On INTC and MSFT,
29\% and 32\% of the test gaps are this short (10 to 14\% on the other stocks), and there the
clamped model reports the highest numbers. Retrained and scored on the actual gaps with the same
1500-epoch budget, it loses 1.1 and 0.7 nats per event and falls below EPT-X, on INTC also below
MSX. For scale, a denser MSX dictionary or sharper EPT-X atoms change the INTC validation
likelihood by at most about 0.05. We report both versions, rank only the corrected one, and include
the patch in the supplementary code.

\subsection{Ablations}\label{sec:ablation}
The results above use one selected configuration per dataset. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows what
each component contributes on validation data.

The two structural parts matter everywhere. Adding the renewal channel improves EPT on every
dataset, and without the Hawkes backbone EPT reaches only \eptNoBackbone{} nats per event on
LOBSTER AAPL, below the classical MSX estimator. The encoder, in contrast, is not the bottleneck.
A causal Transformer encoder \citep{vaswani2017attention} in place of the GRU lowers the validation
likelihood on all five datasets, and a deeper encoder (three residual layers with LayerNorm and
dropout), a wider one (128 units) and S2P2's larger batch bring no gain.

The remaining components are selected where the data call for them. Sharp atoms help where the gap
distribution has sharp peaks: by 1.1 nats per event on Amazon, where a finer grid helps further,
and by 0.05 on INTC. Data-adaptive atoms add to this on Amazon, whose gaps fill two narrow
intervals. The residual mark law helps where the marks follow the elapsed time closely, on Taxi
and StackOverflow. The certified warm start helps most on Taobao and Amazon, and on Retweet it
raises the validation likelihood by 0.03 nats per event. Elsewhere the default setting of each
component is selected.

\paragraph{Where the events come from.} Because every channel has a closed-form compensator, the
expected number of events splits exactly by channel (Table~\ref{tab:attribution}). The split differs
across data. On Taxi, four in five events come from the renewal channel, and on Retweet four in five
come from the neural state. On Amazon, whose gaps are drawn from two fixed intervals, the renewal
channel accounts for all but 0.4\% of them, as a renewal process should. On Taobao and StackOverflow, about half come from the Hawkes backbone,
and on the order books the renewal channel carries 41 to 58\%, with the backbone and the neural state
sharing the rest. The backbone is not constrained to be stationary, so its weights
describe excitation within a sequence rather than a long-run branching ratio.
