\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:gap}]
We use the notation of the proposition: $X$ has rows $\bm x_n^\top=\bm x(t_n)^\top$,
$\lambda_n=\bm x_n^\top\bm\theta$, and
$\ell(\bm\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log\lambda_n-(\bm\Phi+\bm\gamma)^\top\bm\theta$.

\emph{Step 1 (an upper bound for every feasible point).} For $u,v>0$, Fenchel's inequality gives
\[
\log u\;\le\;v\,u-1-\log v .
\]
Take any vector $\bm v=(v_1,\dots,v_N)$ with positive entries such that
$X^\top\bm v\le\bm\Phi+\bm\gamma$ componentwise. Applying the inequality with $u=\lambda_n$ and
$v=v_n$ and summing over $n$,
\[
\sum_{n}\log\lambda_n\;\le\;\sum_n v_n\lambda_n-N-\sum_n\log v_n
\;=\;\bm\theta^\top X^\top\bm v-N-\sum_n\log v_n .
\]
Since $\bm\theta\ge0$ and $X^\top\bm v\le\bm\Phi+\bm\gamma$, we have
$\bm\theta^\top X^\top\bm v\le\bm\theta^\top(\bm\Phi+\bm\gamma)$. Subtracting
$(\bm\Phi+\bm\gamma)^\top\bm\theta$ from both sides therefore gives
\[
\ell(\bm\theta)\;\le\;D(\bm v):=-N-\sum_n\log v_n .
\]
This holds for every feasible $\bm\theta$, in particular for the maximiser, so
$\ell^\star\le D(\bm v)$.

\emph{Step 2 (a computable choice of $\bm v$).} Let $\bm\theta$ be the current fit and set
$v_n=1/(s\lambda_n)$, with $s=\max_p\bigl(\sum_n x_{np}/\lambda_n\bigr)/(\Phi_p+\gamma_p)$. Then
$(X^\top\bm v)_p=\frac{1}{s}\sum_n x_{np}/\lambda_n\le\Phi_p+\gamma_p$ by the definition of $s$, so
$\bm v$ is admissible in Step 1. For this choice,
\[
D(\bm v)=-N+N\log s+\sum_n\log\lambda_n ,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\ell^\star-\ell(\bm\theta)\;\le\;D(\bm v)-\ell(\bm\theta)=N\log s+\bm\theta^\top(\bm\Phi+\bm\gamma)-N .
\]

\emph{Step 3 (the bound vanishes at the optimum).} The Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions of the
maximisation state that $\sum_n x_{np}/\lambda_n=\Phi_p+\gamma_p$ when $\theta_p>0$ and
$\sum_n x_{np}/\lambda_n\le\Phi_p+\gamma_p$ when $\theta_p=0$. Hence $s=1$, so $N\log s=0$. Moreover,
\[
\bm\theta^\top(\bm\Phi+\bm\gamma)=\sum_p\theta_p\sum_n\frac{x_{np}}{\lambda_n}
=\sum_n\frac{\bm x_n^\top\bm\theta}{\lambda_n}=N ,
\]
so the second term is also zero.
\end{proof}

\section{Specification history of the application}\label{app:history}
We report the specifications that failed the built-in falsification test. The final
specification was chosen because it passed this test, not because of the results it produced.

\paragraph{Version 1.} This version used news kernels with time scales up to 30 minutes and Erlang
order 3, a constant baseline in each window and time-of-day effects in UTC. It also allowed tied
events: a trade that crossed several $\delta$ levels produced several events with the same
timestamp. The single placebo type absorbed \vOnePlacebo{} extra events per pseudo-release, which
is comparable to real releases, and the residuals of the crypto series had Kolmogorov--Smirnov
statistics up to \vOneKSmax. Two causes explain this. News kernels longer than the post-release
window absorbed slow trends within the window, and tied events violate the assumption that events
occur one at a time.

\paragraph{Version 2.} This version kept one event per timestamp, with the number of crossed levels
as a mark, restricted news kernels to time scales between 0.1 and 300\,s, used a linear baseline
within each window and modelled seasonality in New York time. The placebo mass fell to
\vTwoPlacebo{} but was still significant. Placebo mass appeared at every release time of day, so
unmodelled 08:30 releases could not be the whole explanation.

\paragraph{Version 3 (final).} Placebo days now also avoid sixteen second-tier US releases. The
news kernels carry an $\ell_1$ penalty, weighted by exposure, whose level is chosen by the
likelihood of held-out windows (Table~\ref{tab:l1}). Without the penalty, nonnegative estimates
inflate release and placebo masses equally. The held-out likelihood peaks at
$\ell_1=\lOneSelected$, which is also where release and placebo masses separate.

\section{Experimental details}\label{app:details}

\paragraph{MSX in the application.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Endogenous kernels: 10 rates from 5\,ms to 600\,s, Erlang order 2.
\item News kernels: 8 rates from 0.1 to 300\,s, Erlang order 3, marks $(1,z^+,z^-)$.
\item Anticipation kernels: 4 rates from 10 to 300\,s.
\item Baseline: a linear trend within each window plus 96 time-of-day hat functions.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{MSX on the benchmarks.} MSX uses one baseline shared by all sequences and a grid of 12
rates from the 1st percentile of the training gaps to five times their 99.9th percentile.
MSX-auto chooses the Erlang order from $\{1,2,3,4\}$ and the $\ell_1$ level from
$\{0,0.01,0.05\}$ on the validation split.

\paragraph{EPT and EPT-X.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Encoder: a GRU with 64 units. Latent state: 4 channels of 8 learnable rates. The number of
Erlang phases was chosen on validation from $\{2,4\}$, together with the hidden size from
$\{64,128\}$, on Taxi, Taobao and StackOverflow.
\item Hawkes backbone: 10 rates with 2 phases each.
\item Optimisation: AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled} with learning rate $10^{-2}$, 1\% linear
warm-up and cosine decay, gradient clipping at 1, and batches of 64 that are halved automatically
if the GPU runs out of memory. Training stops when the validation likelihood has not improved for
40 epochs, within a budget of 300 epochs (25 epochs with batches of 32 on Retweet).
\item Renewal channel: 24 mean gaps spaced logarithmically from half the 0.1\% quantile of the
training gaps to five times their 99.9\% quantile, with orders $\{1,4,16\}$. The mark
distributions start at the empirical mark frequencies.
\item Selected on validation, the LOBSTER streams and Amazon add orders up to 1024 and a
history-dependent shift of the atoms. Amazon also uses a finer grid: the single-seed validation
log-likelihood was 0.604, 0.643, 0.653 and 0.649 with 24, 48, 96 and 192 scales, so we use 96.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Baselines.} The neural baselines run in EasyTPP with the authors' configuration files.
The classical Hawkes estimators use the \texttt{tick} library \citep{bacry2018tick}, with
positive-constrained accelerated gradient descent, tolerance $10^{-10}$ and decay grids matched to
MSX. A short patch, included in the supplementary code, makes the IntensityFree clamp
configurable.

\paragraph{Compute.} All experiments ran on one laptop with an 8\,GB GPU and a 28-core CPU, with
each CPU job limited to four threads. EPT-X runs about as fast on the CPU as on the GPU, because
its event recursion is sequential.
