\section{MSX: certified measurement with echo-corrected responses}\label{sec:msx}

We start with measurement. The aim is a Hawkes model whose fit can be certified and whose response
to a shock, echoes included, is available in closed form. We first state the model, then the
certificate, and then the embedding that gives the response.

\paragraph{Model.} We observe $d$ event types (for example upward and downward price moves of
several assets) inside observation windows $w$, each with a likelihood interval
$[t_0^w,t_1^w)$. Exogenous shocks $e$, here scheduled macroeconomic releases, occur at known times
$\tau_e$. Each shock has a type $c_e$ (CPI, payrolls, and so on) and a nonnegative mark vector
$\bm z_e=(1,z_e^+,z_e^-)$, where $z_e^+$ and $z_e^-$ are the positive and negative parts of the
standardised surprise. The MSX intensity of event type $i$ is
\begin{align}
\lambda_i(t)={}&\underbrace{c_{iw}+\sum_{m}\eta_{im}\,h_m\bigl(\mathrm{tod}(t)\bigr)}_{\text{baseline}}
+\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{n:\,t_n^j<t}\sum_{k,r}a_{ijkr}\,g_{kr}(t-t_n^j)}_{\text{endogenous}}\nonumber\\
&+\underbrace{\sum_{e:\,\tau_e<t}\sum_{k,r,m}b_{i c_e k r m}\,z_{em}\,g^{\mathrm{exo}}_{kr}(t-\tau_e)}_{\text{response to news}}
+\underbrace{\sum_{e:\,\tau_e>t}\sum_{k}\alpha_{ic_ek}\,\beta^{\mathrm{ant}}_k e^{-\beta^{\mathrm{ant}}_k(\tau_e-t)}}_{\text{anticipation}} .
\label{eq:intensity}
\end{align}
The four terms have the following meaning. In the baseline, $c_{iw}$ is an intercept for window
$w$, and $h_m$ are periodic hat functions of the local time of day $\mathrm{tod}(t)$ with weights
$\eta_{im}$. In the endogenous term, $t_n^j$ is the $n$-th event of type $j$, the $g_{kr}$ are the
basis densities~(\ref{eq:basis}), and $a_{ijkr}$ is the weight with which an event of type $j$
excites type $i$ at time scale $k$ and order $r$. The response to news uses a second dictionary
$g^{\mathrm{exo}}_{kr}$ with its own rates, in which $b_{ickrm}$ weights mark component $m$ of a
release of type $c$. Finally, $\alpha_{ick}$ captures activity that builds up before a release of
type $c$, with rates $\beta^{\mathrm{ant}}_k$. All parameters are nonnegative. Every term is linear in its parameters, so we can write
$\lambda_i(t)=\bm x(t)^\top\bm\theta_i$, where $\bm\theta_i$ stacks the parameters of type $i$ and
$\bm x(t)\ge0$ stacks the corresponding known functions of time. The log-likelihood then
separates across event types:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ll}
\ell_i(\bm\theta_i)=\sum_{n:\,u_n=i}\log\bigl(\bm x(t_n)^\top\bm\theta_i\bigr)-\bm\Phi^\top\bm\theta_i,
\qquad \bm\Phi=\sum_w\int_{t_0^w}^{t_1^w}\bm x(t)\,\mathrm{d}t ,
\end{equation}
where $u_n$ is the type of the $n$-th event. Every entry of $\bm x(t_n)$ and of $\bm\Phi$ follows
from Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}.

\paragraph{Concavity and a certificate.} Problem~(\ref{eq:ll}) maximises a concave function over
the nonnegative orthant. We add an $\ell_1$ penalty $\bm\gamma^\top\bm\theta_i$ with weights
$\bm\gamma\ge0$ proportional to each feature's exposure; since $\bm\theta_i\ge0$, the penalty is
linear and simply adds $\bm\gamma$ to $\bm\Phi$. We maximise with the multiplicative EM update
\[
\theta_p\;\leftarrow\;\theta_p\,\frac{\sum_n x_{np}/\lambda_n}{\Phi_p+\gamma_p},
\]
where $x_{np}$ is feature $p$ at the $n$-th event of type $i$ and $\lambda_n$ the current intensity
there. We accelerate it with SQUAREM \citep{varadhan2008simple} and finish with a log-barrier
Newton method. The following bound tells us how far any fit is from the optimum.

\begin{proposition}[Duality-gap certificate]\label{prop:gap}
Fix an event type and drop the index $i$. Let $X$ be the matrix whose $n$-th row is
$\bm x(t_n)^\top$ for the $N$ events of this type, let $\bm\theta\ge0$ be any parameter vector with
intensities $\lambda_n=(X\bm\theta)_n>0$, and let $\ell^\star$ be the maximum of the penalised
log-likelihood $\ell(\bm\theta)=\sum_n\log\lambda_n-(\bm\Phi+\bm\gamma)^\top\bm\theta$. Define
\[
s=\max_p\frac{\sum_n x_{np}/\lambda_n}{\Phi_p+\gamma_p}.
\]
Then
\[
\ell^\star-\ell(\bm\theta)\;\le\;N\log s+\bigl(\bm\theta^\top(\bm\Phi+\bm\gamma)-N\bigr),
\]
and both terms on the right are zero at the optimum.
\end{proposition}
The proof, in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, applies Fenchel's inequality to each $\log\lambda_n$.
Every fit reported in this paper comes with this bound.

\paragraph{Markov embedding.} To study the response to a single release, we collect all
endogenous Erlang states $s_{jkr}$ into one vector $\bm u(t)$ and the states of the release's
news kernels into $\bm v(t)$. Let $F$ be the block-diagonal generator of the endogenous chains
(one block $\beta_k(B-I)$ per source and rate, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}) and $F_e$ the
generator of the news chains. Let $G_{\mathrm{in}}$ be the matrix that adds $\beta_k$ to the first
phase of every chain of source $j$ when an event of type $j$ occurs. Finally, let $\Theta$ hold the
endogenous weights $a_{ijkr}$ and $\Theta_e$ the news weights of this release, so that the excess
intensity is $\Theta\bm u(t)+\Theta_e\bm v(t)$.

Taking expectations, each event of type $j$ occurs at the mean rate $m_j(t)$ and adds
$G_{\mathrm{in}}\bm e_j$ to $\bm u$. The mean excess intensity $\bm m(t)$ caused by the release
therefore satisfies a linear system:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedding}
\dot{\bm z}=\mathcal A\bm z,\qquad
\mathcal A=\begin{pmatrix}F+G_{\mathrm{in}}\Theta & G_{\mathrm{in}}\Theta_e\\ 0 & F_e\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\bm m(t)=C\bm z(t),\qquad C=\begin{pmatrix}\Theta&\Theta_e\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $\bm z=(\bar{\bm u},\bm v)$ stacks the expected endogenous states and the news states, and
$\bm z(0)=\bm z_0=(\bm 0,\bm v_0)$ with $\bm v_0$ the news states just after the release.

\begin{theorem}[Stability]\label{thm:stab}
Let $G$ be the $d\times d$ branching matrix with entries $G_{ij}=\sum_{k,r}a_{ijkr}$, the expected
number of type-$i$ events triggered directly by one type-$j$ event, and let $\rho(G)$ be its
spectral radius. Then the matrix $F+G_{\mathrm{in}}\Theta$ is Hurwitz (all eigenvalues have negative
real part) if and only if $\rho(G)<1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1.} Every block $\beta_k(B-I)$ of $F$ is lower triangular with diagonal $-\beta_k<0$
and nonnegative off-diagonal entries. Hence $F$ is Hurwitz with nonnegative off-diagonal entries,
and $-F$ is a nonsingular M-matrix. The matrix $P=G_{\mathrm{in}}\Theta$ is entrywise nonnegative.

\emph{Step 2.} For such a pair, $F+P$ is Hurwitz if and only if $\rho\bigl((-F)^{-1}P\bigr)<1$. This
is the regular-splitting theorem for M-matrices \citep[Ch.~3]{varga2000matrix}, applied to
$-(F+P)=(-F)-P$.

\emph{Step 3.} The products $AB$ and $BA$ have the same nonzero eigenvalues, so
\[
\rho\bigl((-F)^{-1}G_{\mathrm{in}}\Theta\bigr)=\rho\bigl(\Theta(-F)^{-1}G_{\mathrm{in}}\bigr).
\]

\emph{Step 4.} Column $j$ of $(-F)^{-1}G_{\mathrm{in}}=\int_0^\infty e^{Ft}G_{\mathrm{in}}\,\mathrm{d}t$
contains, for every chain of source $j$, the integral over time of the state left by a single
type-$j$ event. By Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion} this state is $g_{kr}(t)$, whose integral is one.
Hence
\[
\bigl(\Theta(-F)^{-1}G_{\mathrm{in}}\bigr)_{ij}=\sum_{k,r}a_{ijkr}\int_0^\infty g_{kr}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
=\sum_{k,r}a_{ijkr}=G_{ij}.
\]
Combining Steps 2 to 4, $F+G_{\mathrm{in}}\Theta$ is Hurwitz if and only if $\rho(G)<1$.
\end{proof}

The condition $\rho(G)<1$ is the classical stationarity condition for linear Hawkes processes
\citep{hawkes1971spectra,bremaud1996stability}. What Theorem~\ref{thm:stab} adds is that the finite
Erlang embedding inherits it exactly, so the closed-form response, absorption times and
amplification below are valid precisely when the process is stationary.

\begin{corollary}[Closed-form absorption]\label{cor:abs}
Assume $\rho(G)<1$. Then $\mathcal A$ is Hurwitz, because it is block triangular and $F_e$ is
Hurwitz, and the following quantities are available in closed form.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The mean excess intensity after a release, echoes included: $\bm m(t)=Ce^{\mathcal At}\bm z_0$.
\item Its total, the expected number of extra events: $M=\int_0^\infty\bm m(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=-C\mathcal A^{-1}\bm z_0$.
\item The fraction of the response absorbed by time $t$, summed over event types:
\[
\Pi(t)=\frac{\bm 1^\top C\mathcal A^{-1}\bigl(e^{\mathcal At}-I\bigr)\bm z_0}{-\bm 1^\top C\mathcal A^{-1}\bm z_0}.
\]
Its quantiles $t_q=\Pi^{-1}(q)$ are the echo-corrected absorption times; we report $t_{50}$ and $t_{90}$.
\item The amplification $\kappa=M/M_{\mathrm{direct}}$, where $M_{\mathrm{direct}}=-\Theta_eF_e^{-1}\bm v_0$
is the total of the direct news response $\bm m_{\mathrm{direct}}(t)=\Theta_e e^{F_et}\bm v_0$ alone.
\item For an asset whose price is observed through upward and downward crossings of size $\delta$
(types $u$ and $v$), the expected price change up to time $t$:
$\delta\int_0^t\bigl(m_u(s)-m_v(s)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

The usual ``news half-life'' $\ln 2/\beta$ describes only $\bm m_{\mathrm{direct}}$. When $\rho(G)$
is close to one, the echoes dominate the response, and Section~\ref{sec:application} shows that
the two can differ by one to two orders of magnitude.
