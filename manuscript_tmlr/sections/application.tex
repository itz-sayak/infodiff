\section{Application: how fast do markets absorb macroeconomic news?}\label{sec:application}

We now return to the measurement question that motivated MSX and apply it to market data around
scheduled macroeconomic releases. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarises the steps from raw data to
absorption times.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]\centering
\input{figures/pipeline}
\caption{Measurement pipeline. Prices become $\delta$-crossing events, which are grouped into
release windows and matched placebo windows. A single certified MSX fit gives the duality gap and
the placebo test, and its Markov embedding gives the response $\bm m(t)$, the absorption times and
the expected price drift in closed form.}\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Releases.} Our calendar covers 670 scheduled releases between January 2022 and August
2026. It contains CPI, nonfarm payrolls, PPI, retail sales, the GDP advance estimate and initial
jobless claims, each dated by its first vintage in ALFRED, together with FOMC statements and press
conferences, ECB decisions and press conferences, and Bank of Japan decisions. The surprise of a
release is the difference between its first print and a real-time expectation, standardised and
split into positive and negative parts. As expectations we use the Cleveland Fed inflation nowcast
for CPI, the Atlanta Fed GDPNow forecast for GDP, and a statistical forecast from earlier first
prints for the other releases.

\paragraph{Prices and events.} We use millisecond quotes for EUR/USD, USD/JPY, gold and an
S\&P~500 contract for difference, and Binance futures trades for bitcoin and ether. Each price
series is turned into events by $\delta$-crossing: an upward (downward) event is recorded whenever
the log price has risen (fallen) by $\delta$ since the previous event. We choose $\delta$ per asset,
between 0.25 and 3 basis points, so that the median rate is about 0.2 events per second. Each asset
thus contributes two event types, and the twelve types together form the dimensions of the model.
Windows run from 30 minutes before a release to 60 minutes after it, preceded by 30 minutes of
history that is used for the states but not scored.

\paragraph{Placebo windows.} Each release window is paired with a placebo window at the same clock
time on the same weekday, one to six weeks away, chosen so that no modelled release and none of
sixteen second-tier US releases falls within three hours. Placebo windows contain pseudo-releases
of their own type, so the placebo test is part of the likelihood rather than a separate
regression. The pooled model has \nWindows{} windows (\nNewsWindows{} release and
\nPlaceboWindows{} placebo) with \nEventsScored{} million scored events, and its certificate bounds
the distance to the optimum by \gapMaxPooled{} nats per dimension.

\paragraph{Endogeneity.} The fitted model first tells us how endogenous these markets are. The
spectral radius of the branching matrix is $\rho(G)=\rhoPooled$, so
roughly seven in every eight price events are triggered by earlier price events rather than
directly by news. Fitted year by year, $\rho(G)$ declines from 2022 to 2026 (\rhoByYear), and the
slowest echo mode decays in about \relaxPooled{} minutes.

\paragraph{Falsification.} Before reading the news kernels, we test them. Under the null
hypothesis of no effect, a nonnegative news kernel still
has a positive expected mass, because estimates cannot fall below zero. A comparison with
release-free placebo windows is therefore essential. With the $\ell_1$ level chosen by held-out
likelihood, \nPassPlacebo{} of the \nKinds{} release types exceed their placebo by at least 50\%
(Figure~\ref{fig:placebo}): CPI (\placeboCpi$\times$), payrolls (\placeboNfp$\times$), PPI
(\placeboPpi$\times$), the FOMC statement (\placeboFomc$\times$) and retail sales
(\placeboRetail$\times$). Jobless claims, the ECB and BoJ decisions and the press conferences show
no direct effect beyond their placebos. Two earlier specifications failed this test, and we report
them in Appendix~\ref{app:history}.

\paragraph{Echo-corrected absorption.} For the releases that pass the placebo test, the direct
news kernel is absorbed almost immediately: its median $t_{50}$ is \tDirMed{}\,s. The echoes
stretch the response considerably. Half of the extra activity arrives within \tEchoLo{} to
\tEchoHi{}\,s, and ninety percent within \tNinetyLo{} to \tNinetyHi{} minutes
(Figure~\ref{fig:absorption} and Table~\ref{tab:absorption}). The ``news half-life'' $\ln 2/\beta$
therefore understates absorption time by one to two orders of magnitude. Because positive and
negative surprises enter the news kernels separately, the model also gives expected price paths
after a release (Figure~\ref{fig:drift}). A higher-than-expected CPI print lowers bitcoin and
ether, and a lower one lifts them.

\paragraph{Goodness of fit.} These conclusions rest on the fit of the model, which we check with
time-rescaled residuals \citep{brown2002time}. They have
Kolmogorov--Smirnov statistics of at most \ksMax{} against Exp(1); the per-window
excess-dispersion test rejects in \edLo--\edHi\% of windows, compared with 42\% (power-law
kernel) and 93\% (double exponential) of trading days reported for EBS FX data by
\citet{rambaldi2015modeling} with the same test.

\paragraph{Forecasting.} Out of sample, the closed-form MSX forecasts of post-release activity
improve on climatology at every horizon, and its drift forecasts are more informative than the
best baseline (Appendix~\ref{app:forecast}).
